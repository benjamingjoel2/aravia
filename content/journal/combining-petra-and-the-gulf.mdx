Many of our guests arrive in the Gulf for a few days of city and sea, and ask the same question: how far is Petra? The answer is closer than it looks on a map, if the journey is planned well.

## The shape of it

A classic week runs **Dubai, AlUla and Petra**: three nights in the city, three among the canyons of AlUla, and two in Jordan. The connection that makes it work is a short private flight from AlUla to Aqaba, followed by a two-hour drive to Petra. It avoids doubling back through a hub and saves most of a day.

Without AlUla, the simplest route is a direct flight from Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Doha to Amman, then a road journey south. Many guests rest a night at the Dead Sea on the way, which turns the drive into part of the holiday.

## How long in Petra

Two nights, at least. The first afternoon for arrival and Petra by Night; the next morning for the Siq at first light and the Treasury before the day begins; the afternoon for the long climb to the Monastery. A third night allows Little Petra and the high places.

## Adding Wadi Rum

An hour and a half from Petra, Wadi Rum is a different world: red sand, granite and a night sky with nothing in the way. One night in a private camp is enough to understand why people return. From Wadi Rum, Aqaba airport is under an hour away.

## A note on timing

Petra is at its best in spring and autumn, March to May and September to November. The Gulf is at its best from November to April. The overlap, in **November, March and April**, is the sweet spot for combining them.

## Entry

Jordan offers a visa on arrival to many nationalities, and the Jordan Pass can cover both the visa fee and entry to Petra for qualifying stays. Rules change; your concierge will confirm what applies to your passport.
