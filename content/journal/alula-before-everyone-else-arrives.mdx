The road into AlUla runs between walls of sandstone that seem to have been poured and left to set. Before dawn they are grey, then violet, then, for a few minutes, the colour of embers. It is the best hour of the day, and most visitors sleep through it.

## The quiet hours

Hegra, Saudi Arabia’s first UNESCO World Heritage Site, holds more than a hundred tombs carved by the Nabataeans two thousand years ago. Their facades face the open desert, so the low sun reaches them early, one after another, like lamps being lit. In the first hour, you may share the site with a guide, a ranger and the birds.

We plan AlUla days around that light. An early start, a long breakfast after, a rest in the heat of the day, and back out for the evening, when the old town of mud-brick houses fills with people and the cafés open.

## What to see, and in what order

- **Hegra**, at first light, with a guide who can read the inscriptions.
- **The old town**, in the late afternoon, when the lanes are cool.
- **Jabal Ikmah**, an open-air library of ancient inscriptions, in the soft light before sunset.
- **Elephant Rock**, after dark, when it becomes the backdrop to a long dinner.

> Some places reward a schedule. AlUla rewards an alarm clock.

## Staying

The valley has a small number of exceptional places to stay, from villas tucked into the canyons to tented retreats in the dunes. Your concierge will match the house to the way you like to travel: close to Hegra for early starts, or further out for silence.

## When to go

October to March, when the days are warm and the nights cool. The winter festivals bring music and art to the valley; they also bring more visitors, which is one more reason to rise early.
