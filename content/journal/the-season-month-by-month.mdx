The region’s calendar runs in reverse to Europe’s. Summer is for the mountains, the Atlantic and the Mediterranean; the great season begins as the heat lifts in October and lasts until spring. Every date below is a guide only and should be verified each year.

## October and November

The Gulf wakes up. Evenings in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Doha become pleasant, Riyadh Season begins, and the art calendar starts with Abu Dhabi Art. Egypt is ideal, and on or around 22 October the sun reaches the inner sanctuary of Abu Simbel. Late November usually brings Formula 1 to Lusail.

## December and January

Formula 1 closes its season in Abu Dhabi, Dubai’s Shopping Festival begins, and AlUla’s winter festivals fill the canyons with music. In Morocco, the Marrakech film festival and crisp desert nights at Merzouga. Late December brings the Festival of the Sahara at Douz in Tunisia.

## February and March

Kuwait celebrates Hala February. Muscat Nights brings food and performance to Oman’s parks. Marrakech hosts the 1-54 art fair, and Abu Simbel’s first sun festival falls around 22 February. Formula 1 returns to Bahrain and Jeddah in the early part of the season. Spring flowers appear in Jordan.

## April and May

Art Dubai and the Dubai World Cup mark the end of the Gulf season. Petra, Wadi Rum and the Moroccan cities are at their best. In late May or June, Fes holds its Festival of World Sacred Music.

## June to September

The Gulf is hot; the mountains are not. Jebel Akhdar in Oman stays cool, Essaouira’s winds soften the Moroccan summer with the Gnaoua festival in June, and Tunisia’s coast hosts the Carthage festival in July and August. In Jordan, Jerash comes alive with music among Roman columns.

## A note on Ramadan

Ramadan moves about eleven days earlier each year. It changes the rhythm of a journey without making it any less rewarding; read [travelling during Ramadan](/en/travelling-with-grace/ramadan) before you plan.
