Every year, a few guests ask whether they should avoid Ramadan. Our answer is gentle: you need not, and you may come to love it.

## The rhythm of the day

During the holy month, most Muslims fast from dawn until sunset. Days are quieter and working hours shorter. As a guest, you are not expected to fast, but it is courteous not to eat, drink or smoke in public during daylight. Hotels and many restaurants serve guests discreetly, and your concierge will know where.

Then the sun sets, the call to prayer rises across the city, and everything changes.

## Iftar

The fast is broken with dates and water, then a meal shared with family and friends. Hotels, restaurants and community tents across the region welcome guests to iftar, and there are few better ways to understand the generosity at the heart of Arab hospitality. Later, long after midnight, comes suhoor, the meal before dawn, when cafés are full and cities stay awake.

> The month asks for a little understanding. It gives back a great deal.

## Planning around it

We keep sightseeing to the cooler mornings, confirm opening hours that change during the month, and leave evenings free for iftar and the city at its most alive. We plan around Eid al-Fitr early, as flights and hotels fill quickly for the holiday.

## Dress and manner

Dress a little more modestly than usual, keep music and voices low in public during the day, and accept an invitation to iftar if one comes. It is meant warmly.

Expected dates are on our [Ramadan page](/en/travelling-with-grace/ramadan). They depend on the sighting of the moon, so verify them each year.
